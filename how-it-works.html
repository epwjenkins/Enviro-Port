Home / How It Works

One pass. Aggregate out, water reclaimed.

Every Enviro-Port system follows the same core mechanical principle — a returned load goes in, and separated, reusable materials come out. Here's the process from drum to stockpile.

Step 01

Returned mix hits the hopper

A truck backs up to the reclaimer and pours returned ready-mix directly into the intake hopper — no pre-sorting or manual handling required. The hopper is sized to take a full drum discharge without overflow.

Step 02

Multiple trucks, simultaneous discharge

Aggregate Recovery Series systems support multiple discharge points, so more than one driver can empty at once — cutting the time trucks spend waiting at the yard instead of on the road.

Step 03

The screw conveyor separates sand and stone

A material-on-material screw conveyor design carries sand and stone up and out of the slurry. Because the conveyor rides on the material itself rather than metal-on-metal contact, wear on the tank floor and screw housing is dramatically reduced.

Step 04

Gray water flows to the weir settling system

What's left — cement fines suspended in water — flows into a weir settling system. Solids settle out over time, and clarified gray water is ready for the next stage.

Step 05

Recovered aggregate is stockpiled

Separated sand and stone are conveyed to a stockpile, clean and ready for resale or reuse in future batches — recovering material that would otherwise be dumped and hardened.

Step 06 — Complete Recovery Series

Gray water returns to the batch plant

On Complete Recovery Series systems, reclaimed gray water is routed directly back into new ready-mix batches under automated, state-of-the-art control — closing the loop and reducing dependence on settling ponds.

Ongoing

Process & storm water management

Beyond the reclaim cycle itself, Enviro-Port systems help producers manage day-to-day process water and storm water on site — supporting environmental compliance alongside material recovery.

Built for Uptime

Designed to keep running through your busiest pours

Heavy-gauge construction and a material-on-material wear design mean fewer parts in metal-to-metal contact — translating into longer service intervals and less downtime during peak production days.

  • Heavy-duty screw conveyor and hopper construction
  • Multiple discharge points reduce truck queue time
  • Automated controls on Complete Recovery Series systems
